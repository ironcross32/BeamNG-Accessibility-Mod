# Lua: `vehicleScanner.lua`

- **`lua/ge/extensions/vehicleScanner.lua`** — Nearest vehicle detection, target cycling, coupler compatibility matching, alignment teleport. Sends CSV on port 4445, receives commands on port 4448. Range on the 4445 packet is the **surface gap** via `vehicleGeometry`; **bearing deliberately still aims at the target's box centre**, because a bearing to the nearest surface point swings wildly at close range as the winning point hops between corners, and bearing is the number you steer with. **Bearing sign and magnitude MUST come from the same frame.** The angle is measured from `forwardVec` (the implement heading when the override is active) and the left/right sign from `refForwardVec` — the *same* vector, snapshotted before the reverse negation. Deriving the sign from the chassis heading instead is a bug that is invisible on every rigid vehicle and vicious on an articulated one: the two frames differ by the articulation angle, so for a target near the *cab's* centreline the magnitude is the articulation angle while the sign flips, and the reading snaps ±30° without passing through zero. Measured at a 60.1° single-tick jump against 0.1° once fixed; `vehicle_geometry_sim.lua` scenario 10 asserts both. `refUpVec` follows the same rule — world up with the flattened implement heading, chassis up with the chassis heading. **`toTargetVec` is also flattened**, or three metres of boom travel shrinks the dot product and inflates the bearing with no yaw change at all, i.e. lifting the bucket appears to steer the machine. Coupler distance mode is untouched by any of this — it already does node-to-node with `rearOverhang`/`frontOverhang` compensation and already assumes reversing, so a generic flip layered on top would double-count.
  - **Every vehicle name is resolved GE-side, through `describeVehicle` → `vehicleNaming.describe`.** It used to be a cross-VM round trip that read `v.data.information.name` in the vehicle VM. BeamNG localized that field: it is no longer a string but `{txt = "ui.vehicleconfig.information.name.BRAND", ctx = {brand1 = "ui.vehicleconfig.brand.Ibishu Pessima"}}`, and **the vehicle VM has no translator at all** — `_tr` is installed only by the GE-side `core_locales` (which is why `lua/common/jbeam/io.lua`, shared by both VMs, guards its own use with `and _tr`). So the round trip could only ever come back with a two-entry table, `normalizeSpeechValue` rightly refused to speak it, and every vehicle fell through to the JBeam basename — a Pessima announced as "midsize". The GE side needs no round trip and no translation of its own, because `core_vehicles.getVehicleList()` has already run the model name through `_tr`. `normalizeSpeechValue` now also *translates* the `{txt, ctx}` shape via `core_locales.translateWithOrWithoutContext` rather than logging a warning and giving up, so any other localized field that reaches it reads correctly. Part names inside the vehicle VM (the detached-part list on `DAMAGE`) genuinely cannot be translated there and fall back to the path-derived `friendlyName`, which is why that fallback exists.
  - **The coupler align parks you SHORT and hands over; nothing in this mod couples for you.** `F9` `Shift+V`'s scanner alignment used to branch on coupler type: a ball hitch got a gap and the reverse-to-couple homing tone, a fifth wheel got a teleport onto the king pin followed by six `beamstate.activateAutoCoupling()` calls 0.3 s apart. That branch could never have worked, in three independent ways, and its failure mode was a confident lie.
    - **`beamstate.activateAutoCoupling()` cannot couple a fifth wheel.** The T-series' `fwk2` carries `couplerLock = true`, and `activateAutoCoupling` builds its visible tag set from `not couplerWeld and not couplerLock and couplerTag` — so it excludes the fifth wheel and arms only the `pintle`. The trailer side is worse: a stock `tanker` has **zero** `couplerTag` nodes (712 nodes, 0 matches) and its king pin `kingp3` carries only `tag = "fifthwheel_v2"`, read by the game's `couplings/kingpin` controller. The real attach is done by `couplings/fifthwheel.lua`'s `updateGFX`, which needs the two vehicles **physically colliding** (`mapmgr.objectCollisionIds`) and the king pin within `couplerRadius * 0.8` = **0.08 m**. Pressing L only toggles a marker. So the mod parks you square and short and the driver reverses, which is what the ball hitch always did — the coupler type now selects only the *axis source* and the *standoff*, never a behaviour.
    - **A zero-gap align is a request the game refuses, and refusing is not what it does — it RELOCATES.** Asking for the fifth wheel exactly on the king pin puts the truck's oriented box inside the trailer's, so `spawn.safeTeleport` → `placeVehicle` → `placeVehRec` searches outward for clear space. Measured twice, identically: shoved **4.43 m back and 1.26 m sideways**, leaving a 4.725 m gap to an 0.08 m coupler and destroying the squareness the key exists to produce. **There is no gradient to lean on**: once the first `isIntersecting` fails, every retry candidate is built at `getHalfExtents() * 2`, so the search is for a spot clear of a box eight times the truck's volume. Either the requested pose passes the un-inflated test and you land exactly where you asked, or you land metres away — which is why the clearance needs real margin and why the landing is **verified** (`ALIGN_DISPLACE_SAY_M`) rather than assumed. That check is the whole reason the old bug was invisible: a request the game quietly declined read exactly like one it honoured.
    - **The standoff is derived from the two bodies, because it is a property of the PAIR.** How far the truck reaches behind its own fifth wheel (1.680 m) plus how far the trailer reaches ahead of its king pin (0.866 m) is where the boxes touch — 2.546 m for this pair, so the ball hitch's fixed 1.5 m would still have overlapped. Note the boxes are what matters, not the bodies: a fifth wheel works precisely *because* the trailer nose overhangs the tractor frame and they never touch. Only the align cares, because only the align goes through `isIntersecting`; the driver reversing is unaffected. The overhangs ride back on the ALIGN discovery chunks the round trip was already making, rather than through `vehicleGeometry` — its extents are gap-trimmed and would be better, but they are an **async** resolve with a `failed` terminal state, and an align that silently fell back to the zero-gap form would restore the exact bug. The raw sweep can only *over*-estimate (a detached part inflates it), which parks you further back — the safe direction — and is clamped by `ALIGN_OVERHANG_MAX_M` so a wreck cannot park you in the next county.
    - **The align centres the COUPLER on the target's axis, not the reference node.** They are different points: the T-series' fifth wheel sits **0.434 m** off its own reference node laterally, so the old form left it a third of a metre to one side of an 0.08 m target. The offset is measured along `vec3(0,0,1):cross(fwd)` and cancelled along `vec3(0,0,1):cross(awayDir)` — **world** up on both sides, because both forwards are flattened and the two have to be built identically or the cancellation is partial on a vehicle sitting nose-up. `quatFromDir(awayDir, up)` sets the truck's forward *to* `awayDir`, so the correction subtracts; getting that sign backwards doubles the error to 0.868 m and looks identical from the seat, so it is asserted rather than reasoned about. Measured after the fix: **0.001 m** off axis. Same correction `RAMPALIGN` already carries, applied to a different point. The field it replaced (`nx`) was the raw world-axis x of the node offset — a lateral offset only on an axis-aligned vehicle — and was transmitted but never read by anything.
    - **The coupler-tracking bearing is FLATTENED, and that is not cosmetic.** It is a steering error, and both plates sit about a metre up, so any height difference between them was being reported as horizontal error — at 0.3 m separation a 0.15 m height difference is 27 degrees of phantom steering command, with a sign taken off an almost-vertical vector and therefore flipping per tick, in the last half metre of the manoeuvre. Same rule the scanner bearing already follows for the implement boom. `leftVec` keeps the **un-negated** forward, as everywhere else: the driver's physical left does not move when they select reverse.
    - **Nothing announces a coupling it has not observed.** `COUPLER_ATTACHED` was sent purely because the retry counter ran out, with no attachment check — it said "Fifth wheel coupled" while `fifthwheel.isAttached()` returned false. It is deleted at both ends; `COUPLED_DETECTED`, driven by the real GE `onCouplerAttached` hook, is the only success announcement. That hook does reach us for an *external* coupler: the engine calls the vehicle VM's global `onCouplerAttached` (`lua/vehicle/main.lua:384`) for any attach, which forwards GE-side from `beamstate.lua:430` under `objectId < obj2id` — hence the handler tests `playerInvolved and targetInvolved` rather than assuming an order. Note `beamstate.isCouplerAttached()` keeps reporting **false** for a coupled fifth wheel, because both its loops skip `couplerLock` nodes; assert on the controller or the hook, never on beamstate.
    - **The coupler is chosen by ROLE, and the player and target chunks are MIRRORS of each other.** A coupler tag is a coupling *standard*, not a role: `fifthwheel_v2` is carried by the plate and the pin alike. A `log_trailer` has both — its own king pin at the front (`fwdOffset +7.193`) and a fifth wheel at the back (`-3.075`) for pulling a second trailer — and the name ladder scored them identically, leaving `pairs()` order to pick. It picked the rear plate, so the align aimed the truck **10.27 m** behind the real king pin and reversed it into the trailer. A `tanker` had always worked by luck: it carries no `couplerTag` node at all, so its king pin won by default, which is precisely why the resolver looked sound. The rule is now a capability check on the thing that does the coupling: `couplings/fifthwheel` owns the **towing** end and names its node in jbeam, `couplings/kingpin` owns the **towed** end and names its own — so the player chunk asks the first and the target chunk asks the second. Same shape of argument `rampGeometry.isCannon()` makes, and it needs no allowlist of trailers. Measured after the fix, on a `log_trailer`: 0.0024 m off axis.
      - **The ball-hitch half of the ladder had the identical bug, spelled with two tags instead of one shared one**, and it is fixed by the same mirror rather than by a special case. A `tow_bar` is the drawbar you hook up *to*; a `tow_hitch` tows something else. Both chunks ranked `tow_hitch` above `tow_bar` — correct for the truck, backwards for the trailer — so a trailer carrying a rear hitch as well as its own drawbar resolved to the hitch. The target chunk now ranks the **towed** end first and the player chunk is untouched; a trailer with only a drawbar resolves identically under either ranking, so nothing that worked can change.
    - **A coupling run outlives the scanner toggle, and the tracking block therefore sits ABOVE the scan-mode gate.** Silencing the scanner's periodic "169 feet, behind you" callouts is not a request to abandon a coupling; it is what a driver does *because* they are concentrating on one. Below the gate it was, and the `OFF` handler additionally cleared `couplerAttachMonitor`, so a toggle ended the run in two ways at once and switching the scanner back on re-armed neither -- only a fresh `ALIGN` does. Observed on the first real coupling: aligned, scanner toggled off and on four seconds later, coupled nineteen seconds after that with **no homing tone for the reverse and no "Coupled" afterwards**, both failures silent. This mattered little while the fifth wheel had its own auto-coupling path; once that was deleted the whole manoeuvre rides on tracking. A run now ends only when it is actually over -- coupled, target lost, or a vehicle switch. Python needs its own `coupler_run_active` latch for the same reason: with the scanner off the Lua feed is still live, so clearing the tone in `toggle_scan_mode` would silence the instrument while its own packets were still arriving.
    - **The landing gear retracts itself, but not through the controller you would first find.** A `tanker` has no `controller/trailerFeet.lua`; it uses `linearActuators/linearActuatorTrailerFeet`, whose `onCouplerAttached` drives the actuator to `attachedDefaultPosition` (nil, i.e. 0 = up) from a `detachedDefaultPosition` of 0.75. Verified after a real coupling: `actuator1.currentExtendPercent` = 0.015. So the deleted `electrics.values.feet = 1` was redundant either way -- but check the actuator, not the electrics value, when confirming it.
    - **The landing gear is the game's job, and the trailer brake is nobody's.** The deleted block also poked `electrics.values.feet = 1` and zeroed the trailer's brakes. The first is redundant — `controller/trailerFeet.lua` retracts the gear itself on `onCouplerAttached`, in the trailer's own VM, off the same dispatch. The second was never verified to stick and is a behaviour nobody asked for: releasing the parking brake on a trailer you have just coupled to is how it rolls away. Both are simply gone rather than moved.
  - **Reverse moves the contact set to the rear band and negates the reference heading**, so a target dead behind reads ~0° rather than ~180°. `GEAR:R`/`GEAR:F` is pushed from `beamtel.py` on change (it already decodes gear from the telemetry struct; cheaper than a cross-VM electrics poll), aged out after `GEAR_STALE_SEC` and backed by a velocity-along-forward fallback — gear is preferred because at a standstill about to reverse the readout should already be rear-referenced, and velocity reads zero there. **The resolved direction then rides back on the 4445 packet as a fourth field, and Python's speech reference reads it from there** — not from the gear it pushed. Mirroring the push locally is wrong in precisely the two cases where Lua ignores it: after `GEAR_STALE_SEC` it is on velocity instead (roll backwards down a slope in D and the bearing is rear-referenced while Python still believes forward, so speech says "in front of you" about what you are reversing into), and a vehicle switch clears it outright. The field is optional on the wire — the parse is positional with a `len(parts) >= 2` guard — so an older mod simply falls back to the pushed gear.
  - **`playerLeftVec` must keep being built from the UN-negated forward vector.** It is `up × fwd`, so negating `fwd` silently negates the cross product and reports left as right for as long as the vehicle is in reverse. The driver's physical left side does not move when they select reverse; positive stays LEFT in every gear. `vehicle_geometry_sim.lua` scenario 8 asserts it *and* asserts that the buggy form would invert, so the check cannot pass for free.
  - **The implement override is scoped to forward only.** Phrasing the rule as "the implement supplies the *forward* contact set" rather than "loaders use the implement" is what makes reversing a loader fall out for free — it drops to the rear band like any other vehicle, with no loader-specific code. Backing up measured from the bucket was the single worst case in the old build: the whole machine length of error, in the wrong direction.
