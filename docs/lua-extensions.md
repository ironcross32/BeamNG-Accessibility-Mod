# Lua: Assorted GE Extensions

- **`lua/ge/extensions/beamtelAI.lua`** — AI mode control (chase, follow, flee, stop). Receives commands on port 4449.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/obstacleDetector.lua`** — 12-ray fan obstacle detection + terrain drop-off/hill sampling. Speed-scaled ranges. 4-quadrant slots prevent audio cacophony. Port 4452→4453.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/cameraInfo.lua`** — Camera spatial data (yaw, pitch, AGL). Port 4450→4451.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/terrainScanner.lua`** — The polar snapshot behind the terrain sonification scan. Port 4471→4472. Passive until a `SCAN` command arrives, then it samples a `SCAN_BEARINGS` × `SCAN_RINGS` grid (24 × 25 = 600) across the forward 180° out to `SCAN_MAX_RANGE_M`, tags water, collects nearby objects, and sends one datagram. It **only gathers** — every decision about how any of it sounds lives in `audio.py`'s `render_scan`.
  - **Visible geometry is authoritative.** Every sample starts with a top-down `be:getSurfaceHeightBelow` ray, which sees TerrainBlocks, GroundPlanes, static meshes, bridges, and other rendered collision surfaces. Only a failed ray falls back to `core_terrain.getTerrainHeight`; this prevents Gridmap's hidden TerrainBlock from shifting the scan and object pings down by roughly 44 metres. The reference elevation under the vehicle uses the same surface-first rule. Every ray, including a second water-bed probe when a flat water surface is the first hit, spends one of `SCAN_RAYS_PER_TICK`'s 100 slots. A normal 600-cell scan therefore collects over roughly six frames (occasionally more over flat water), adding about 100 ms before the reference ping while staying well inside the response watchdog.
  - **"No surface" is `~`, and must never be `0`.** Zero is level ground. Reporting a plateau where the map ends is the one error here that produces confident, entirely plausible output.
  - **The reach ceiling is the terrain's own SIZE, not the distance to the nearest edge.** The game exposes no render/draw-distance scalar to Lua at all — a scan of the whole engine `lua/` tree finds only per-view `farClip` values and `$pref::Terrain::lodScale`, neither queryable — so the terrain block's extent is the honest analogue of "or the rendering distance, whichever comes first". Deliberately not the distance to the nearest edge: shrinking the whole scan because one flank runs out of map would compress the time axis in every *other* direction too, and Python scales its entire time axis by the reach reported here. Samples that fall off the map answer `~` individually, which is the right answer for them. `SCAN_MAX_RANGE_M` must match `audio.py`'s; `terrain_scan_sim.py` scenario 11 greps this file and asserts it.
  - **Water is spatial, not one global elevation.** Water objects are resolved once at scan start. `WaterBlock` cells must fall inside that block's XY footprint, while an unbounded `WaterPlane` is global only for its own surface elevation. `River` objects are world-bounds-prefiltered and then queried with `containsPoint()` immediately below the sampled visible surface; the containing segment's node elevation and depth are interpolated to recover the bed. A bridge or other surface above the water volume therefore stays dry while exposed water beside it is tagged. Water remains `dz_depth` on the wire even when depth is exactly zero (`dz_0.0`); dry ground remains `dz`, and a missing surface remains `~`.
  - **Objects come from `be:getObject` only.** In BeamNG most "props" — cones, barriers, haybales, pallets — *are* spawned vehicle objects, so this is the right set. `TSStatic` is enumerable but excluded, because on a stock map that is thousands of trees and building shells and burying the terrain bed under a wall of pings makes the instrument useless.
  - Bearings are built from `vec3(0,0,1):cross(fwd)`, the mod-wide positive-is-LEFT convention, and the sim greps for it. A new `SCAN` supersedes one still gathering rather than finishing it first, which would send a snapshot of where the vehicle used to be. `M.diag()` reports detected water objects by class plus the last completed scan's reference elevation, visible-surface range, water/dry/missing counts, and total rays — the evidence needed when a geometry resolve otherwise produces a plausible wrong answer.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/cannonShot.lua`** — What happened to the car you just fired out of the stock `large_cannon`. Port 4473, **send only**. Not an aiming instrument: the cannon has no horizontal aim to give, and where a soft-body car thrown by a hydraulic ram lands is not derivable — so this reports the *outcome* instead, which costs nothing extra because the car has to be reset and re-driven into the ramp regardless. It also accumulates the one thing that would make prediction possible later, an (elevation, strength) → observed-range row per shot, gathered from shots fired for their own sake.
  - **Detection is observational, never hooked.** The mod uses `onGameplayEvent` nowhere and has no visibility of the cannon's `controller:large_cannon` fire trigger; going and getting one would tie this file to *how* a shot happens to be triggered. A shot is latched when the player's vehicle is inside the mouth's throat and its speed **along `mouthFrame.axis`** crosses `LAUNCH_MPS` — you enter a ramp at walking pace and leave the barrel at tens of m/s, so the band between them is enormous. Same rule that makes `actualSteering` and the jacking detector work, and the same one `implementProximity` already follows in deriving `CANNON:` by polling geometry rather than by event.
  - **A cannon is `rampGeometry.isCannon()`, never `has()` — and that distinction is what stopped this file reporting shots nobody fired.** `has()` means "you can drive into this", which is true of every rollback, tilt deck and dry van the part tiers resolve, and while the launch watch was gated on it each of those was a machine being watched for a launch *out of*. A parked `us_semi_rollback_deck` resolves a 9.24 m ramp whose throat these gates cover to 25 m along and ±1.9 m lateral, so **driving past one at road speed inside that corridor satisfies every launch condition** and latches a shot on a car that was never in a cannon. Exactly the correction `implementProximity`'s `CANNON:` line already had to make, for exactly the same reason.
  - **The scan is paced in REAL time; the shot is aged in SIMULATED time.** Two clocks on purpose. A flight's duration, its settle window and its timeout are facts about the *car*, so they are measured in the time the car experiences; the 20 Hz scan rate is a fact about the machine running the game. Ageing the flight on `dtReal` means a pause or a slow-motion — **opening the radial menu does the latter** — runs the 30 s timeout down while the projectile hangs motionless, and the shot is announced as "did not settle" having never had the chance to. Worse, a paused vehicle reports the pose *and the velocity* it had when the pause began, so the launch conditions stay frozen true and the thing **re-latches on the next tick and does it again, every thirty seconds, for as long as the menu is open** — eight in a row, all 30.0 s apart, are in the log this was found in. Hence both halves: `watchForLaunch` declines a tick with no simulated time (stops it latching) and the flight ages on `dtSim` (stops it ageing). `vehicleSpawnerAccessible.lua`'s copy of the detector carries the same fix, and `cannon_shot_sim.py` greps for all of it alongside the three shared constants.
  - **The mouth frame is SNAPSHOTTED at launch, never re-read.** The assembly retracts and re-levels within a couple of seconds of firing, so a landing measured against the live mouth is measured against a machine that has since moved — and the elevation, which is the whole range-card key, would be read after it had already gone.
  - **Elevation comes free from `mouthFrame.pitchDeg`.** Ramp and barrel are on the same tilting assembly, so the ramp's live pitch *is* the elevation — no bore-node resolve, no name matching, nothing new. It is **not** the bore angle (the two sit at different angles on the assembly) but it is monotonic with it and exactly repeatable, which is all a range-card key has to be. Strength is the only figure not derivable geometrically and comes from one `queueLuaCommand` at launch reading `electrics.values.gear`, the percentage string the cannon's controller hijacks — built by concatenation with no `string.format`, so that percent sign stays data.
  - **Distance is measured from where the car WAS when it was launched**, not from the mouth. That is the datum a person means by "how far did it go", and unlike the muzzle it needs no extra resolve.
  - **The settle detector is a deliberate COPY of `vehicleSpawnerAccessible.lua`'s `updateLaunchTrack`, not an extraction.** The two answer different questions — that one decomposes a miss against an intended target, this one reports position along a firing axis — and the mod already duplicates small helpers on this reasoning (`groundHeightBelow` lives separately in `cameraInfo.lua` and `vehicleSpawnerAccessible.lua`, "standalone by design"). What must not happen is the three constants drifting apart, so `cannon_shot_sim.py` greps both files and asserts they are identical.
  - **A shot that never settles reports as "did not settle" and quotes NO figure.** The car is still falling, still rolling, or wedged on its roof; reporting where it happened to be when the timer ran out states a landing place for a flight that did not land — the one error here a listener has no way to catch, because it reads exactly like a real result. A mid-flight reset or vehicle switch abandons the shot for the same reason.
  - **Target PLACEMENT needs no code at all.** Mark the cannon in the accessible vehicle spawner and pick **Line / "Anchor at front"**: `frameFromVehicle` builds its frame from the jbeam `refNodes`, all four of which are `base_*` and therefore `{"fixed":true}`, so the anchor is welded to the world and — measured in game — does not rotate when the barrel elevates. Downrange is the cannon's *backward*: `refNodes` declares `back:` toward +y while the muzzle is at +y, so `getDirectionVector()` points out through the ramp mouth (`dot = -1.000` against `mouthFrame.axis`). Verified over 600 m: spacing exact to 0.1 m, lateral drift 0.0. The anchor sits ~9 m short of the muzzle and the batch's own first item spawns *at* the anchor, i.e. inside the cannon.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/environmentAccessible.lua`** — The environment values the stock pause screen has no control for, as a list Python renders as a virtual browser (`F9` `N`). Port 4474→4475. Today that is the ambient temperature; the row protocol is named-field so the next one is a row rather than plumbing.
  - **Temperature is not a settable value in BeamNG — it is DERIVED, every frame.** `core_environment.onUpdate` interpolates the level's temperature *curve* against the current time of day and pushes the result into the engine with `be:setSeaLevelTemperatureK`. `core_environment` exports `getTemperatureK` but its setter is a file-local, so writing a temperature directly is overwritten on the next frame. That is also why the pause UI has panels for wind, clouds and fog and none for this: those are values, this is an output. The only durable way in is to change the curve the loop reads — `theLevelInfo`'s `temperatureCurveC`, a list of `(timeOfDay, °C)` pairs, which has a real engine setter (`setTemperatureCurveC`).
    - **`core_environment.onInit()` is the refresh, and `setEditorDirty()` is the trap.** `onUpdate` only re-reads its cached curve when the LevelInfo reports editor-dirty, so `setEditorDirty()` looks like the cheap way to prompt it — but it is a no-op outside the editor: measured in game, the flag reads back `false` and the temperature never moves. `onInit` is `reset` under the hood, re-reads unconditionally, and is exported. It takes effect on the FOLLOWING frame, not within the call, which is the whole reason the state resend is deferred by `RESEND_DELAY_S` — resending immediately reports the value that was just replaced.
    - **A flat set writes TWO curve points, never one.** `onUpdate` returns early on a curve with fewer than two points, so a single-point curve is a set that reports success and leaves the temperature frozen wherever it was.
    - **The level's own curve is captured before this extension has ever written one**, per level and dropped on level change, so `RESTORE` puts back what the level author wrote rather than a flat guess — including for the *first* edit of a session, which is why the capture is on `onClientStartMission` rather than lazy.
    - **A curve that is not flat has no single temperature, and the row says so.** The figure the browser shows is a point on that curve, true only for the current time of day, and setting one replaces the author's whole day cycle with a constant. `curveLo`/`curveHi` ride on the row so Python can announce `varies 41 to 77 through the day`; without it that loss is silent. Same reasoning for `editable`: `core_environment.canChange()` is false inside missions and scenarios, and a write there appears to succeed and is reverted by whatever holds the lock, so it is refused and named instead.
    - **Rows are named fields (`k=v;k=v`), not positional CSV.** `bng_mod/` is a live junction into the game install, so the two halves genuinely do go out of step; this project has already paid for positional tails twice (the extended telemetry struct and the `DOCK:` line). A field one side does not know is ignored here rather than shifting everything after it — `environment_row_sim.py` asserts exactly that with a future `humidityPct` field.
  - **The browser key is `N`, not the obvious `E`.** Plain `E` is already "Speak engine temperature" and its branch sits earlier in beamtel's F9 chain, so an `E` binding here would have been unreachable — dead code that reads as a working feature.
  - **Left/right step in the unit the user HEARS.** The wire is always Celsius; stepping the Celsius value by 1 moves an imperial readout by 1.8 °F, which skips numbers and makes 60 °F unreachable no matter how the key is pressed — a control that responds to every press and cannot be aimed. `diagnostic/environment_row_sim.py` (`python diagnostic/environment_row_sim.py`) lifts the real functions out of `beamtel.py` by AST rather than copying them, and asserts five presses give five consecutive numbers.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/trailerAngle.lua`** — The yaw between the vehicle you are driving and the trailer hooked to it. Port 4476, **send only**. Not a new instrument: the angle is normalised onto the WL-40's own `actualSteering` scale and drives the *same* tone, so a driver who has learned one has learned both. A tractor and its trailer are an articulated machine with the hinge moved to the coupler, and the failure mode is what makes it worth sonifying at all — a jackknife is completely silent until it is unrecoverable.
  - **The registry is READ every tick, never hooked.** `vehicleScanner` already implements `onCouplerAttached` and it is precisely the wrong shape for this question: it fires once, at the instant of coupling, and it is gated on `couplerAttachMonitor`, which is armed only between an `ALIGN` and the first matching attach. A trailer coupled any other way — drove up and pressed L, a save reloaded, a career session resumed — is invisible to it forever, and `onCouplerDetached` is log-only. What this needs is not an event but a continuous fact, and the game maintains exactly that GE-side in two places the mod had never touched: `core_vehicles.attachedCouplers` (a flat `{objId1, objId2, nodeId, obj2nodeId}` list, maintained on attach/detach/destroy and **serialized across reloads**, so it survives the save/load case a hook cannot see) and `core_trailerRespawn.getTrailerData()` (already *directed*, tractor → trailer, so it needs no geometry to say which end is which). The directed one is preferred and the pair list is the base, because `core_trailerRespawn` can be switched off (`setEnabled(false)` nops its own hooks) while the pair list cannot.
  - **Exactly ONE trailer is reported, and it is the one hinged directly to you.** That is the joint that folds into the cab and the one the driver can still do something about; a second continuous channel for the trailer behind the trailer is the mistake the obstacle detector already made once. Where the pair list offers a choice — a truck being towed while towing — the partner **behind** the player wins (most negative along the player's forward), because you jackknife what you are pulling, not what is pulling you.
  - **The yaw is FLATTENED before it is measured, and that is not cosmetic.** The quantity wanted is swing about the hitch; an unflattened dot product folds pitch into it, so a trailer dead in line but nose-up on a kerb, cresting a hump or squatting under load reports several degrees of phantom swing — and the silence the whole feature is built around never arrives. At 12° of pitch the naive form invents 12° of yaw, which is five times the deadzone. Same rule the scanner bearing and `getImplementFrame` already follow. A frame that collapses to residue when flattened (a vehicle on its roof) does **not** degrade gracefully — normalising residue picks a direction per tick and the reported side then flips at random — so `lastGoodFwd` is held instead, exactly as `implementProximity` does, and is dropped per vehicle on reset and destroy.
  - **Sign is `vec3(0,0,1):cross(fwd)`, the mod-wide positive-is-LEFT convention**, and `TRAILER_ANGLE_SIGN` exists so the one calibration that only the driver's seat can settle is a single named constant rather than a negation buried in an expression. `796F6C6F313035.lua`'s `STEER_ARTIC_SIGN` is there for the same reason. `trailer_angle_sim.lua` scenario 2 asserts the `fwd:cross(up)` form comes out exactly backwards, so the check cannot pass for free.
  - **`TRAILER:CLEAR` is a message, not an absence.** "Nothing is coupled" and "the mod stopped talking" are different facts, and Python must not have to infer which from a timeout — a timeout is the failure path, and a jackknife tone left on because the failure path was the only way to turn it off is the worst outcome this feature has. Every giving-up path routes through it. Python **also** ages the value out (`TRAILER_STALE_SEC`), because a crashed extension or a version skew ends the feed with the last angle still standing; the mod's heartbeat is set well under half that so a live, perfectly steady reading can never expire. Rounding happens *before* the change test, or a trailer breathing on its springs re-sends forever while parked.
  - **The tone is ducked going forward, not gated.** Forward, a trailer straightens itself out of every corner, so an un-ducked tone would sound on ordinary driving and be trained away as noise long before the one manoeuvre it exists for. Ducked rather than silenced because a trailer genuinely can be pushed out of line going forward — a tight turn against a kerb, unloaded on camber — and going quiet there would teach that silence means straight when it does not. Reverse is taken from the **gear**, not from velocity, for the reason `_push_gear_direction` gives: at a standstill about to reverse the instrument should already be at full volume, and velocity reads zero there.
  - **Python's source selection is `actualSteering == 0.0` exactly**, which is a wire contract and not a tolerance dodge: `796F6C6F313035.lua` sets it to a hard `0.0` on any vehicle whose cylinder and hydro scans both come up empty, precisely so "no such thing" stays distinguishable from "straight". Every ordinary car therefore falls through to the trailer source and the WL-40 path is untouched. A loader towing something reports its **own** frame bend — that hinge is closer, moves faster, and is the one that folds the machine.
  - **The centre-crossing click disarms on a change of source.** Uncoupling a trailer sitting at an angle drops the value straight to 0.0, which is a crossing into the deadzone by every test the click applies — so it would fire and announce "straight" about a trailer that is no longer there, at the one moment the driver has just felt it come off. The click means "this hinge arrived at centre", so it may only fire when the hinge it is tracking is the one it was tracking last block.
  - Send-only, so it binds no listening socket and is outside the `setsockname`/`onExtensionUnloaded`/`retryCmdBind` contract the fourteen listening extensions share — `cannonShot.lua` makes the same argument. Note that stating that fact in the header is itself a hazard: `vehicle_geometry_sim.lua` scenario 12 greps for `setsockname` and originally failed this file for *documenting* the rule, which is why that scenario now skips comment lines the way scenario 13 already did.

- **`lua/ge/extensions/uiToggle.lua`** — Hides/shows the game UI on command (`ui_visibility.set`/`toggle`, the same thing ALT+U toggles). Used by the AI Describer to remove HUD elements before a screenshot. Port 4464.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/consoleAccessible.lua`** — Screen-reader-accessible equivalent of the stock ImGui developer console (the backtick `` ` `` console, which exposes no accessibility tree). Re-implements its execution model: runs commands in the GE Lua (via `executeLuaSandboxed` for captured return + print output), GE TorqueScript, CEF/UI JS, and per-vehicle Lua contexts; enumerates those contexts on demand; and can stream the global engine log (`Engine.getFrameLog()`). Surfaced through native wx controls on beamtel.py's Main tab. Port 4465→4466.
- **`lua/ge/extensions/vehicleBindings.lua`** — Vehicle-specific control bindings, the data behind the stock "Special Vehicle Keys" app (`ui/modules/apps/Keys`), which is pure Vue and has no accessibility tree. Port 4467→4468; surfaced as a virtual browser on F9 then B. Rebuilt silently on every vehicle load — **it must never speak**, or it would talk over the spawn.
  - The list is a join the game does not expose as one call: iterate `core_input_bindings.bindings` → `device.contents.bindings`, look up `core_input_actions.getActiveActions()[binding.action]`, keep `cat == "vehicle_specific"`, drop `binding.unused` and `core_input_actionFilter.isActionBlocked()`. This mirrors the unexported `getVehicleSpecificActions()` in the game's `lua/ge/extensions/ui/bindingsLegend.lua`, except that function discards `binding.control` and we keep it. **Filtering on `cat` is also the part-config capability check** — `core/input/actions.lua` resolves usability against `vdata.actionsEnabled` and demotes anything the current config cannot do to `vehicle_specific_unusable`, so a Pickup without a locker correctly shows no Toggle Rear Diff. Driving the list from `bindings` rather than from the action table is also what makes it bound-only.
  - Same `vdata`-not-ready trap as clickspots: `onVehicleSwitched` fires before `vdata.inputActions` exists, which is what feeds `getActiveActions()`. Hence the same retry harness (0.5 s × 10) plus a `be:getPlayerVehicleID(0)` poll in `onUpdate` as a backstop. An **empty** result is treated as "still loading" until the retry budget runs out, because that is indistinguishable from "no special controls" at the first attempt.
  - Key names are prettified **in Lua**, which the game does not do anywhere: `CONTROL_LABELS`/`controlLabel()` live only in `ui/ui-vue/src/services/controls.js` and compress for display with glyphs (`PG↑`, `L⇧`, `NP0`) that a screen reader cannot read. `KEY_LABELS` is that mapping expanded for speech instead, modifiers joined with `" plus "`, keyboard given no device prefix (`L`, not `keyboard L`) since it is the common case. Devices are read keyboard-first — the reverse of the stock `DEVICE_ORDER` — so a listener can stop after the key.
  - Enter fires the action through `core_input_actions.triggerDownUp`, i.e. the same ActionMap path a real key press takes, so onDown/onUp pairing stays correct. Unlike the clickspot browser this needs no delayed press/release thread on the Python side.
