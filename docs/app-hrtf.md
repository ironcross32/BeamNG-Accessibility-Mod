# App: HRTF, gear reports and wheel slip

`beam-base` `gear_report.rs`, `beam-reports` `wheel_slip.rs`, `beam-audio` `hrtf.rs` and `beam-base` `hrtf_sets.rs`. Ported from the Python modules of the same names.

- **beam-reports wheel_slip.rs** — Wheel slip (spin / lockup) and side-slide detection, plus where the voices are placed. `F9` `Ctrl+K`. Pure module like `beam-base` `route_beacon.rs`: `beam-engine` steps a `SlipDetector` per telemetry packet, and `beam-road` `road_diagnostics.rs` and `beam-road` `challenge_results.rs` call its stateless `classify()` on recorded snapshots, so the live tone and every report run one rule. `wheel_slip_sim` (`beam-audio`, `beam-reports`) replays a real Ice Pool recording (`diagnostic/data/slip_ice_etkc.csv`) — run it after touching any of this.
  - **The voice's pitch is the slip RATIO, measured against MotionSim, never the wheel-derived speed.** The game's speed readout comes from the wheels, so a car parked with its wheels spinning reads 50 mph. `slip_ratio` divides the divergence by the faster of wheel and ground speed, so it stays inside 0..1 without dividing by a standstill: spinning in place or fully locked is 1.0 and the top of the range (`SLIP_PITCH_RISE_OCT`, one octave above the carrier's base). The absolute divergence still sets the volume, up to the configured `slip_tone_level_dbfs`, which is the ceiling. It could not set the pitch, because 10 m/s of spin at 60 mph and 10 m/s from a standstill read the same.
  - **The wheel signal is the raw per-wheel extreme, never `electrics.values.wheelspeed`.** That value (still sent as `speed` for everything else) is a smoothed average of *every* wheel. Measured on ice it trailed a 30 ms spin-up by ~0.4 s and read 4.9 m/s with the rears at 21 and the fronts at 0.2 — averaging halves one-axle spin as well as delaying it. The vehicle VM now appends `wheelSpeedMaxDriven` and `wheelSpeedMin`; a packet without them (`-1`) drops the detector onto the exact legacy rule rather than into silence.
  - **Spin has no ground-speed gate; lockup does.** The old rule required the car to be doing 2 m/s before anything counted, so a clutch dump on ice — wheels at 12 m/s, car creeping — was reported about a second late, after the driver had already lifted. A wheel outrunning a stationary car is unambiguous. At a standstill every wheel "looks locked", so lockup keeps the gate. Recorded latencies: 0.97 / 0.95 / 0.25 s before, 0.08–0.10 s after, and a brake lockup the old rule reported only *after* the wheels released is now reported while they are locked.
  - **Wheels are compared with LONGITUDINAL travel only**, `ground * cos(travel bearing)`. Against total ground speed a sideways slide reads as lockup, because lateral motion is speed the wheels never see. Sideways travel is its own voice: the **slide**, the angle between travel and the car's axis (either end), with 12°/8° hysteresis above 3 m/s so a drift held near the threshold does not chatter.
  - **The voices sit toward the direction of travel**, from the MotionSim velocity through `relative_bearing` (which carries the matching half of BeamTel's 180° heading offset — do not re-derive it). Within 10° of the car's axis it snaps dead ahead or dead behind; below walking pace, where the direction is residue, the gear decides. `beam-audio` smooths the azimuth as a **unit vector**, so a slide swinging through dead astern stays behind instead of sweeping across the front.
  - **The voices carry harmonics to 5 kHz, or the HRTF cannot place them.** A 90 Hz sine has almost no interaural or pinna cue, so ahead and behind would be indistinguishable. Spin (220 Hz sawtooth) and lockup (90 Hz, odd harmonics only) keep their pitch identity as band-limited wavetables; the slide is band-passed noise, crossfaded darker-to-brighter with the angle, so it cannot be confused with either tonal voice and a power drift reads as two layers from one point.
- **beam-base gear_report.rs** — Gear-change reporting, pure like `beam-reports` `wheel_slip.rs`: `beam-engine` decides *when* a gear changed and calls `report_gear_change`, which speaks or plays per `gear_report_mode` (`off` / `speech` / `audio`, migrated from the old `announce_gear` boolean by `migrate_config`). `gear_report_sim` (`beam-base`) asserts all of it — run it after touching this.
  - **The default speech is byte-identical to what came before the module**, and the sim's first scenario asserts it. The number style (`gear_speech_number_style`) touches only plain numeric forward gears; `sport 3` / `manual 5` were already numbers. It applies to change announcements only, not the `F9` gear readouts.
  - **Sport collapse (`gear_speech_sport_once`) keys on the previous gear string, not on a timer.** Entering any `S`/`S<n>` gear from a non-sport one says "sport"; `S<n>`→`S<m>` is silent in both directions. The telemetry does not say whether the gearbox is a CVT, so it applies to every sport gear — which is the point: an automatic in sport also shifts on its own. It is **one setting for both modes** (the settings panel shows the one checkbox under either "As speech" or "As audio"; the key keeps its old `gear_speech_` name so existing configs carry over): in audio, entry plays one bright tone at the entered gear's degree and the steps inside sport are silent. `beam-engine` gates the whole `report_gear_change` call on `sport_step_silenced`, so speech and tone cannot drift apart.
  - **A manual gearbox's neutral is deliberately never reported, in speech or audio.** It arrives as a bare `"0"` (manual and sequential boxes send `gearbox.gearIndex` as the gear name; automatics send `"N"`, which is reported). In BeamNG's "realistic" manual mode, pressing a shift button drops the box into neutral on the next frame and holds it there until the button is **released** (`shiftLogic/manualGearbox.lua`, the `waitingForShift` state), so reporting `"0"` doubles every shift. The game offers no usable signal: `electrics.values.isShifting` is **false** during that held neutral, and the state machine is local. Two fixes were considered and rejected by the user: a settle timer (tried before, disliked), and wrapping `controller.mainController.shiftUpOnDown`/`OnUp` to hold the previous gear while a shift is pending (it works for buttons, but H-pattern shifters still pass through a real neutral). Do not re-add neutral for manuals without asking. OutGauge cannot tell the box types apart (byte 1 is neutral for all of them), so its path still reports neutral.
  - **A gear's tone is a scale degree counted from neutral**, which sits on the chosen root: P −2, R −1, N 0, D and first +1, then one step per gear, wrapping octaves through the scale. `S<n>`/`M<n>` take `<n>`'s degree. Scale steps are float semitones, so the quarter-tone scales (chromatic, Rast, Bayati) share the arithmetic. Strings with no degree (the cannon's `80%`) are silent.
  - **The tone is a half-cosine onset, a short hold and a cubic decay** (`TONE_ATTACK_MS` / `TONE_HOLD_MS` / `TONE_DECAY_MS`), four harmonic partials so it stands out over engine noise, normalised to an exact peak of `gear_tone_level_dbfs` (−3 by default). The cubic tail is 18 dB down by its midpoint, so it reads as short.
  - **Sport gears play the same pitch, brighter** (`TONE_PARTIALS_SPORT`: six stronger partials, centroid about 1.5x). Drive and sport 1 share a degree, so without it D→S1 is the note just heard repeated and the mode change is inaudible. Timbre rather than pitch, because the pitch still has to say which gear it is. Same peak level; the sim asserts brighter but under 2x. Rendered on the telemetry thread by `trigger_gear_tone`; the callback only mixes a list of in-flight buffers, so a quick double shift overlaps rather than cutting a tail mid-way (a click).
- **beam-audio hrtf.rs** — HRTF binaural panning. Loads one baked set (`.npz`, read by `npy.rs`), processes it into a single ring at the configured elevation on a whole-degree azimuth grid, FFT-resamples to the output rate, and serves `get_hrir(azimuth)` as a linear blend of the two nearest entries. Used by `beam-audio` for every spatialised cue. The `.npz` files are committed. They were baked from their `.sofa` sources by the Python `bake_hrtf.py`, which went with the Python application (git history before "Remove the Python application"; the SOFA download URLs are in it): the Rust only reads them, so rebaking a set or adding one needs that script.
  - **Which sets exist is `beam-base` `hrtf_sets.rs`, and nothing else.** `BUNDLED_SETS` drives the settings panel's *HRTF Dataset* dropdown and `hydro_steer_sim` (`beam-audio`)'s width check, and is the list of files a release must ship beside the exe (`beam_base::paths::find_data_file` looks there, then in the repository root); the packager should read it rather than repeat it. The config key `hrtf_set` stores the **id**, never the label, and `resolve()` maps an unknown id to the default, so a stale or hand-edited config cannot leave HRTF unloaded. `hrtf_elevation_deg` goes through `clamp_elevation`; the elevation range and the defaults for both new settings live there too. Imported sets, if they are ever added, append to `available_sets()`.
  - **Shipped:** MIT KEMAR (default; rings every 10°, 5° azimuth steps, 44.1 kHz, unequalised — thin and harsh as measured), Neumann KU100 from TH Köln (`HRIR_FULL2DEG`: rings every 2°, 2° azimuth, 48 kHz, 128 taps; the same collection's `HRIR_CIRC360` is horizontal-only, so it cannot give elevation) and SADIE II D2 KEMAR (rings at 0, -15, -17.5, -25, +15 — **nothing between 0 and -15** — 48 kHz, 256 taps, diffuse-field equalised, approximately linear phase). The baker keeps every ring in `ELEVATION_MIN_DEG..MAX_DEG` plus the nearest one beyond each end, about 6.5 MB for all three. Licences and attribution are in `HRTF_DATA_LICENSES.md`, which has to ship next to the exe (the release packager must copy it); the KU100 files are **CC BY-SA 3.0** per their own metadata (Zenodo says CC BY), so the notice has to ship with them.
  - **Why the processing exists: blending raw HRIRs comb-filters.** Neighbouring measurements arrive at slightly different times, so the old sample-by-sample blend between 5° KEMAR entries put a 7.2 dB ripple into the in-between angle (47.5°). `process_ring` splits every measurement into an ITD and a magnitude response, interpolates those separately (within each ring, then between the two rings either side of the elevation), and rebuilds each whole degree as a minimum-phase filter delayed by its half of the ITD. Whole-degree neighbours then differ by under half a sample, and `get_hrir`'s blend deviates at most 0.4 dB from them.
  - **The ITD comes from the low-passed (1.5 kHz) interaural cross-correlation, not an onset threshold.** SADIE's responses are linear phase and ring before their main peak, so a threshold reads garbage there. Expect about 700-760 us at 90°; a curve that jumps between neighbours means the estimator has picked the wrong correlation lobe.
  - **Left and right are forced to mirror images.** Dummy heads are symmetric by design; the measured asymmetry (head placement) pulled dead ahead about 1 dB to one side on the KU100 and SADIE sets, which every "centred" cue (line-up, docking) would inherit.
  - **Equalisation (`hrtf_equalise`, on by default) flattens what every direction shares**: the ring's mean power response, smoothed to 1/3 octave so the direction-dependent notches survive, limited to +/-12 dB and held flat outside 150 Hz-12 kHz. On MIT KEMAR that is about +11 dB below 500 Hz and -7 dB at 2 kHz — the measurement loudspeaker's missing bass and the ear-canal resonance. SADIE barely moves (already equalised), KU100 moves a dB or two. Above 12 kHz MIT KEMAR asks for the full +12 dB, which would only lift measurement noise; that is why the band stops there.
  - **Loudness is matched to the unprocessed MIT KEMAR ring.** Every cue level in `beam-audio` was tuned on it. `band_energy` is the mean |H(f)|^2 over all positions and both ears in 300 Hz-8 kHz; it averages over frequency, so it does not depend on sample rate or IR length. `REFERENCE_BAND_ENERGY` is that measure on the original 72-position file.
  - **Elevation is honest about what it can do.** Near straight ahead people blur elevation by roughly 10° or more, and a non-individual dummy head makes height cues weaker still, so the default of -3° mostly changes timbre slightly. Within the SADIE gap, -3° is 80 % the 0° ring and 20 % the -15° ring.
  - **Switching is live and costs a few hundred ms.** `apply_live_config` calls `AudioController.select_hrtf_set(set, HERE, elevation, equalise)` on every reload; it is a no-op unless that triple changed, and it runs on the config watcher thread, never the audio callback. The new set is built completely, then swapped in with one assignment. Nothing needs resetting: every overlap-add tail adds `min(len)` samples and the line-up crossfade checks IR length. A set that fails to load leaves the current one playing. Processed IRs are about 540 samples at 48 kHz (10 ms of minimum-phase body plus the delay), roughly what the old 557-sample KEMAR cost per convolution.
  - **The ILD curve differs per set, and some `beam-audio` constants were tuned against KEMAR's.** `HYDRO_STEER_MAX_AZIMUTH_DEG` (75°) still passes for all three, but on the KU100 the ILD rises only ~0.4 dB over the last quarter of lock, against ~1.6 dB on KEMAR.
