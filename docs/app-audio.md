# App: beam-audio

The synthesis engine and every voice (`crates/beam-audio`). Ported from the Python `audio.py`; the reasoning below was worked out there and the Rust keeps the same constants and behaviour. Sims are Rust tests: `cargo test -p beam-audio --test <name>`.

- **beam-audio** — Procedural audio synthesis engine. Real-time stereo 48kHz float32 via a `cpal` output callback (`callback/`, one file per voice; the controller is `controller.rs`). Compass clicks, shift/TC/steering tones, warning buzzers, drift alerts, heading guidance, scanner beeps, and a single predictive obstacle cue. The obstacle voice uses its own 413.7 Hz even-harmonic spectrum, one cadence clock, an emergency doublet/steady latch, and selective guidance ducking; terrain and other safety warnings remain outside that duck bus. See [obstacle-warning.md](obstacle-warning.md).
  - **Articulation / hydraulic steering tone** — for machines whose steering does not self-centre (the WL-40 wheel loader bends its frame with a pair of opposed rams, so releasing the joystick leaves it bent). Driven by `actualSteering`, which is **0.0 on every vehicle with normal self-centring steering** — that is what auto-detects the hydraulic ones, so nothing here needs a per-vehicle check. Pitch (`HYDRO_STEER_LO_HZ`→`HI_HZ`) and level (`MIN_DB`→`MAX_DB`) rise with the offset, HRTF azimuth tracks it out to `HYDRO_STEER_MAX_AZIMUTH_DEG`, and the voice is **FM** rather than a sine because the loader itself is loud enough to bury one. The C:M ratio glides from an exact 2.0 near straight (sidebands land on harmonics — clean) to a non-integer 2.76 at full lock (off-harmonic — rough), with the modulation index rising alongside, so being badly off-centre *sounds* wrong rather than merely higher. Ratio and index follow `norm ** HYDRO_STEER_FM_DIRT_CURVE`, **not** `norm` — scaling them linearly is piercing in normal use, because half lock already lands at ratio 2.38 and a 1.5 kHz centroid. Cubed, the ratio holds within ~0.1 of the integer through the middle of the range and only goes wild in the last quarter. Pitch stays linear in `norm`; it's only the timbre that's curved. **`HYDRO_STEER_MAX_AZIMUTH_DEG` must stay well under 90°** (75° now): the KEMAR set's interaural level difference peaks near 67.5° and then *falls* about 5 dB out to 90°, so mapping full lock to 90° made turning harder past ¾ lock read as the sound coming back toward centre. The sim asserts this. Note the azimuth scales on raw `actual_steering` while level and pitch scale on the `HYDRO_STEER_FULL`-normalised value, so the stereo image keeps opening after the tone has saturated. Carrier and modulator phase, ratio and index are all glided across the callback block; stepping any of them per-callback zippers. and a **centre-crossing click** fires once as the frame arrives at straight — silence alone is ambiguous, since it also means "idle". That click is deliberately *atonal* (seeded band-limited noise, 2.5–7 kHz): the compass and cam clicks are FM tones around a 900 Hz carrier, so anything pitched near there gets mistaken for one. Band-limited rather than plain high-passed, because raw noise peaks near Nyquist — thin, and the first thing to go with high-frequency hearing loss. The click arms only past `HYDRO_STEER_DEADZONE * HYDRO_CENTER_REARM` so hunting around centre can't machine-gun it. Level uses a dB *range*, not a linear scale to zero, or the near-straight case would be inaudible — which is the case the feature exists to report. `hydro_steer_sim` (`beam-audio`) replays the gate/envelope/click state machine against a fake clock — run it after touching that block.
  - **Loader implement tones** — two voices for the WL-40's bucket/forks, both driven off the implement block in the extended struct and both gated by one shared fade envelope. Both are **split left/right by a fixed equal-power pan** (`IMPL_STREAM_PAN`, ground left, tilt right). They were originally both centre-panned, reasoning that the articulation tone already owns the HRTF slot, the implement is dead ahead by definition, so an azimuth of 0 carries no information and would cost two convolutions per callback for nothing. That was true and beside the point: **two continuous sounds from one point in space fuse into one object** — the same principle this file invokes to justify rendering the docking beat pair centre, running the other way. The two voices are continuous, and they overlap in register (the 180 Hz carrier sits 1.6 semitones under the tilt scale's 200 Hz floor and its sidebands land across the middle of it), so centred they were reported as *one tone that changes character*, with lift and tilt indistinguishable. The split is **not a direction cue, it is a streaming cue** — a constant offset cannot be misread as a bearing because a bearing moves and this does not — and being pure gain it costs two multiplies, not the convolution a real azimuth would. `implement_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) scenario 6c asserts the mirror and the equal-power sum. `implement_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) and `implement_proximity_sim` (`beam-engine`) replay both state machines against a fake clock — run them after touching either block.
    - **Ground proximity (FM).** Grit means "close to the ground"; pitch means "you are levering the machine off its wheels". Keeping those on two axes is the whole design, which is why the carrier is **fixed during the approach** — a rising pitch can then only ever mean lift. The C:M ratio starts on an exact integer far out and drifts off it as the implement nears the ground, with the index rising alongside. **`IMPL_GROUND_RATIO_DRIFT` must stay below 0.5**: at 1.0 the drift would arrive at the *next* integer exactly at contact, so the tone would sound clean at the moment it means "about to hit" — the inverse of its meaning. The dirt curve is **2.0, not the articulation tone's 3.0**: there, grit is an alarm reserved for the last quarter of travel, so cubing is right; here the drift *is* the graded distance readout and has to be legible from about a metre out, and cubed it leaves 2.0 m → 0.5 m nearly clean. When the implement is actually against the ground and jacking the machine up, a smoothstep ramps the ratio onto the next integer (clean again) and the carrier rises by `IMPL_GROUND_JACK_OCTAVES` with the lift. **`IMPL_GROUND_INDEX_HI` and `IMPL_GROUND_MAX_DB` were both pulled back after play-testing** (3.2→2.2 and −16→−19.5 dB, together about a quarter off the brightness at contact). The original values assumed near-the-ground was a state you pass *through* on the way to a dig; getting a bucket **under** something means working there continuously, and at the old settings the tone was harsh enough to be a reason not to use the feature. The level is only a default — `implement_ground_tone_dbfs` overrides it, so an existing config keeps whatever it already had. **Both implement tones map their level as a span hanging below the configured value** (`IMPL_GROUND_SPAN_DB`, `IMPL_TILT_SPAN_DB`), not as an interpolation up from `IMPL_*_MIN_DB`. The MIN constants are the quiet end *at the default level*, not an absolute floor: the old form inverted as soon as the configured level fell below one of them, and the settings panel allows −120, so a user turning the ground tone down to −40 got −30 dBFS two metres out and −40 at contact — quieter as the bucket approached, loudest with nothing to report. `implement_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) scenario 15 asserts monotonicity at −45 and −80 and that the default is unchanged.
    - **Tilt (quarter-tone, pure sine).** A sine rather than FM so it is instantly distinguishable against the two FM voices, and because steps are easiest to count on an uncluttered spectrum. 400 Hz is level — genuinely level since the tilt axis became the implement's floor plane rather than a diagonal across it (see `implementFlags` below); the scale's code did not change but its *meaning* did, and it was previously reporting level at an angle nothing can enter. ±24 quarter-tone steps reach 200 Hz and 800 Hz at the implement's **real** mechanical stops, which are learned since the last restart (falling back to `IMPL_TILT_FALLBACK_SPAN_DEG` so it is usable on the first lift). **A running extreme is not a mechanical stop, and using it as one was the "first tilt after a load snaps, then it's fine" bug**: while the implement is still travelling outward the running max *is* the current angle, so `tilt/span` is exactly 1.0 and the step pins to ±24 for the whole stroke — measured at a 22-step jump in one tick, then pegged, going sane only once a real stop had been passed. An extreme is promoted to a stop only after the implement retreats `IMPL_TILT_STOP_CONFIRM_DEG` off it, and the newly learned span is swapped into the scale only while within `IMPL_TILT_SPAN_SWAP_DEG` of level — the one place every span agrees on step 0, so the rescale is inaudible; adopting it at angle jumps the readout by however much the fallback over-estimated the real travel (~9 steps on the WL-40). `implement_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) scenario 6b asserts both, and asserts that the old form pegs. The two ends are not equidistant from level, so the map is two-sided about a shared zero. Linear degrees → logarithmic frequency is what makes the steps evenly *perceived*; a linear-Hz map bunches them badly at the bottom. The frequency is one-poled with `IMPL_TILT_GLIDE_TAU`, **never `linspace`d across a block to the new value** — that turns a discrete step into portamento and destroys the countability the scale exists for — and every step change fires a 6 ms notch, which is what makes a fast sweep countable rather than one continuous slide.
    - **The shared gate** fades both tones out ~1 s after the implement stops moving and re-attacks in ~30 ms. There is **no hold timer**: `implementActivity` already folds in the ram's travel *rate*, so a momentary joystick release mid-lift keeps the gate open on its own. The ground tone's gate additionally opens on **closing rate** (`IMPL_GROUND_CLOSING_MPS`), not just on actuation — driving forward into a pile with a motionless bucket is the implement's position changing relative to the ground, and gating purely on joystick input would go silent during exactly the approach the tone exists to report. While the gate fades, the tilt step is **frozen** at its last actuated value, or a bucket settling under gravity re-steps mid-fade and reads as though it were still being commanded. The articulation tone is ducked `IMPL_HYDRO_DUCK_DB` while the gate is open, using the previous callback's gate value (that block runs first) — one buffer of latency on a 4 dB change, i.e. inaudible.
  - **Docking instrument (`DOCK_*` constants, `dock_*` state)** — the loader alignment tones, gated by `F9` `Ctrl+I`. Two voices for three dimensions: the panned pulse carries lateral on the stereo image and range on its repetition rate, the beat pair carries vertical. `dock_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) replays the gate/phase/lock state machine against a fake clock — run it after touching that block.
    - **Ramp mode reuses all four voices and changes only constants.** `update_dock_target(range, lateral, null_value, mode, acquire, bearing_deg)`. **The mode travels with the values, not on `set_dock_mode`**, because the two arrive on different threads (the F9 keypress vs the 10 Hz listener): a mode change sitting one packet ahead of its own scaling puts 24 Hz/m through a figure the other mode's slope was sized for, which saturates the beat and fires a spurious lock chime — the instrument announcing "aligned" at a moment nothing is. On a mode change `dock_lock_armed` and `dock_beat_env` are **reset**, because the arm latch is hysteresis on a quantity whose scale has just changed by an order of magnitude; `dock_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) scenario 13 asserts zero chimes across the switch and asserts the un-reset form fires one. `DOCK_RAMP_LATERAL_FULL_M` is 3.0 against the implement's 1.2 because on a ramp approach you are routinely three metres off centre at fifteen out, and at 1.2 the pan would be pinned hard over through the whole acquire phase — the one phase in which the pulse is the only voice and the pan is carrying everything. `ramp_resolve_sim.lua` scenario 10 now asserts the audio-vs-mod range invariant, which was previously only a comment.
    - **In ramp mode the null channel is LATERAL, not yaw** (`DOCK_RAMP_BEAT_HZ_PER_M`, 2.0). It carried yaw originally, which contradicted this instrument's own reasoning: the implement readout deliberately keeps squareness *off* the continuous channels because "it only matters at the instant of entry", and ramp mode then sonified exactly that while leaving the one axis you must nail to fit between two walls on the pan alone. Reported from the seat as *"the tones don't really let me know where I am side to side"*, which is what an instrument with no null channel on its most important axis sounds like. Lateral now arrives three ways that reinforce: the **pan** says which side, coarsely and spatially; the **beat** says how far off, finely and self-calibrating, chiming on the null; the **tremolo rate** says which side again, categorically — and that last one is the only one of the three that survives on speakers, where a 700 Hz pulse's low-frequency stereo image largely does not. Fast (`DOCK_AM_ABOVE_HZ`) means the centreline is to your **right**; arbitrary, so it is asserted in `dock_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) scenario 12 rather than remembered. The slope sets the lock window as always: 2 Hz/m → chime at 0.25 m, re-arm at 1.25 m, comfortably inside the ~1.2 m of margin a car has in a 2.15 m half-width mouth. Yaw moved to the spoken tap, where the implement readout already puts it. `null_value` is ignored in ramp mode and the pair is driven from `lateral_m` **inside** `update_dock_target`, so there is exactly one lateral figure and the two voices cannot be handed different ones.
    - **The ramp cane tap is two numbers and then only what is wrong.** Cut back hard after the same play-test ("too much verbiage"): it used to open with `Cannon ramp, Large Cannon standard white` on every press — twelve syllables identifying a machine you are already looking at — and then read four facts whether or not any needed acting on. Now the target is named **once, on acquisition**, by the mode announcement (`Ramp alignment, <machine>`, re-fired on a target change and latched under the same rules as `last_dock_mode`, i.e. never cleared by `DOCKCLEAR`); the tap itself is `<offset> <side>, <range> <unit>` with the unit spoken once, and squareness (`RAMP_DOCK_YAW_SAY_DEG`, 8°), clearance and ramp pitch appear **only as bad news**. The word "square" is gone entirely: nothing in this readout confirms that things are fine, because that is what the tones are for.
    - **Ramp mode has two phases, and they answer different QUESTIONS with the same three channels.** Lateral offset and squareness are corrections to a line you are already on; from beside or behind the machine there is no such line, and reading them out there ("four metres right, 123 degrees left") gives an operator nothing to do, because what they need first is simply *where the mouth is*. So outside the approach corridor the pulse becomes a beacon: its rate follows the straight-line distance to the mouth (`hypot` of the signed along-range and the lateral, derived in `update_dock_target` rather than sent, so it cannot disagree with its own terms) and it is panned at the **true bearing to the mouth over the whole circle** — deliberately *not* squeezed into `DOCK_MAX_AZIMUTH_DEG`, which is a front-hemisphere ceiling appropriate to a steering error, when the whole point of this phase is that the mouth can be behind you. The null channel is **held out entirely**, both because the "offset from the centreline" of a line you are not on is most of the width of the machine — nulling it drives you into its flank — and because the pair arriving as you come into the corridor is then itself the "you are lined up on it now" cue. `ramp_acquire` is latched once per packet in `beam-engine`'s listener — one authority for the tones and the spoken readout, atomic with the numbers by construction — and is hysteretic (`RAMP_CORRIDOR_ENTER_M`/`EXIT_M`) because the boundary swaps what the pan *means*. Losing the target, a part swap or a vehicle change all reset it to hunting. `dock_readout_sim` (`beam-engine`) scenario 17 and `dock_tone_sim` (`beam-audio`) scenario 14 assert both halves, including that the old floored range pinned the pulse at its contact rate from twelve metres out.
    - **The hunting phrase names a place, not a correction.** Bearing first then distance (`82 right, 9.9 meters`), plus `wrong side` when you are past the mouth plane, and the lateral/squareness/clearance clauses are dropped outright — the clearance one is actively misleading off the line, since the margin is the mouth's half-width minus your *current* offset and reads as "too narrow by three metres" about a car that fits. The bearing is `atan2(lateral, range) + yaw`, exact rather than approximate, and derived in beamtel for the same reason the distance is.
    - **The two clauses of the hunting phrase are in different reference frames, so only one of them may use a direction word.** The bearing is measured from the driver's nose; the mouth-plane clause is measured against the ramp. Both are routinely true at once — aimed squarely at an entrance you have already driven past is an ordinary state while hunting — and wording the second one `behind` produced `ahead, 73.9 feet. behind`, which is not contradictory but reads exactly as though it were. `wrong side` cannot be mistaken for a bearing. It also fires on the **sign of the range** rather than on `RAMP_CORRIDOR_MIN_RANGE_M`: a car half a metre short of the mouth plane is in the doorway, and the threshold called that wrong side too. `dock_readout_sim` (`beam-engine`) scenario 17 sweeps 280 geometries asserting no utterance ever carries two direction words, and asserts the old wording collides.
    - **It replaces rather than adds.** The scanner block is suppressed outright while the dock gate is open and the articulation tone is ducked by `DOCK_HYDRO_DUCK_DB` (−12, far harder than the implement's −4). Not a mix decision: articulation says what your input *did*, the docking pan says what you *need*, and on a final approach they are the same axis twice — the goal wins. Both read the *previous* callback's `dock_gate_env` since the dock block runs last; one buffer on a level change is inaudible. The articulation centre-crossing click is untouched, so the frame reference survives.
    - **Phased by range**, so no more than three dimensions are ever live: silent past `DOCK_MAX_RANGE_M`, pulse only in to `DOCK_BEAT_RANGE_M`, pair fading in inside that. The pair has its own envelope rather than sharing the gate, or the acquire phase would be three quiet dimensions instead of two real ones. At four metres the vertical genuinely does not matter and you are still steering; three channels there is how the obstacle detector became something the user never switched on.
    - **Vertical is a beat pair nulling to unison, not a pitch.** Self-calibrating (no reference frequency to learn), the ear resolves a beat far finer than a pitch, and it is texturally unmistakable against the two FM voices and the pure-sine tilt scale. Three things about it are corrections to a first version that failed in listening — it was reported as *"one tone that stutters and raises and lowers in pitch"*, which is precisely what that version produced:
      - **Both partners move, symmetrically about `DOCK_REF_HZ`.** Holding one fixed and sliding the other moves the pair's *average*, and two tones that close fuse into a single pitch percept sitting at that average — so the listener hears a tone gliding, with the beating masked behind it. Symmetric placement nails the centre pitch so the only thing that varies is the beat. Measured: 330.1 Hz centre across the whole error range.
      - **The detune is specified as a beat rate in Hz** (`DOCK_BEAT_HZ_PER_M`, `DOCK_BEAT_MAX_HZ`), not as an interval in cents. Cents makes the audible result a function of `DOCK_REF_HZ`, and the original 200-cent cap was ~40 Hz apart at 330 Hz — far outside the range where beating is heard as beating at all. **Past roughly 15 Hz of separation the percept turns into roughness and then into two separate pitches**, so the cap has to stay under it.
      - **The pair is rendered CENTRE, not summed into the panned pulse.** Two sounds from one point in space fuse into one object. Splitting them is also honest — the vertical null is not a directional quantity — and free, since centre needs no convolution, the same argument the ground and tilt tones already make.
      - The tremolo sign carrier multiplies the **sum**, not one partner: modulating a single tone adds sidebands and muddies the very beat the pair exists to produce.
    - **Beating is signless**, so above-versus-below rides on a tremolo whose *rate* differs (`DOCK_AM_ABOVE_HZ`/`DOCK_AM_BELOW_HZ`), suppressed inside the lock window — a steady unison is the clearest available "stop".
    - **The unison lock chime is an armed latch inside the callback**, like the articulation centre-crossing click: hysteresis (`DOCK_LOCK_CENTS`/`DOCK_LOCK_REARM_CENTS`) on a continuous quantity, fired from the callback so it is sample-accurate and cannot machine-gun while hunting the null. Silence cannot report a null on its own — a smooth tone is also what "nothing to say" sounds like. It is a *descending two-note* figure because every other slot is taken: FM around 900 Hz (compass/cam clicks), a noise burst (articulation centre), single sines near 1 kHz (scanner/coupler). `DOCK_LOCK_WAVEFORM` must be regenerated at **both** build sites, as `HYDRO_CENTER_CLICK_WAVEFORM` is.
    - Only the pulse is user-levelled (`dock_tone_dbfs`); the pair sits at a fixed offset from it, or a mix exists in which the null cue is inaudible under the range cue.
  - **Terrain sonification scanner (`SCAN_*` constants, `render_scan`)** — a one-shot picture of the landscape rather than a warning about it, fired by `F9` `Space` while the world is live and gated by `scan_tones_enabled`. The obstacle detector says what you are about to hit and the docking instrument says what you are lining up with; neither tells you that you are in a valley, on a ridge, or beside a lake. `terrain_scan_sim` (`beam-audio`) asserts every property below — run it after touching any of it.
    - **The whole scan is pre-rendered on the LISTENER thread, not synthesized in the callback**, and that is the load-bearing decision. Six hundred overlapping grains evaluated per 512-frame block blew the Python callback's budget, and would still be a large, bursty load for the Rust one, and take *every other voice in the mod* down with it, not just this feature. There is nothing to gain by deferring, either: the scan's length is fixed and its content is fully determined the moment the packet lands. The callback does nothing but `bufL += self._scan_buf[pos:pos+frames, 0]` and advance a cursor. Being pure is also what lets the sim assert on the instrument with no stream, no device and no clock — every other audio sim in this project needs a fake clock; this one needs nothing. It reads the buffer **before** the cursor and clamps, because `play_scan` can swap them from another thread between the two reads and a new buffer indexed by an old position is the one way that block can throw. Measured at ~35 ms to render a full 600-sample scan.
    - **Three mappings on three different axes, so none has to share a channel.** PITCH is elevation, TIME is distance, PAN is bearing. Level explicitly carries *nothing* — the user asked for time rather than volume to encode distance, and doubling it up on level as well would only make the far half of every scan quiet for no added information.
    - **Pythagorean, not equal temperament, and level ground is middle C.** A landscape is full of repeated intervals — a hillside at a steady grade lands on the same step over and over — and a 12-TET fifth is 2 cents narrow, which at this register beats at about 2 Hz. Pure 3:2 and 4:3 do not beat at all, so a constant slope reads as a stable chord instead of a wobble. `SCAN_PYTHAGOREAN_RATIOS` is the chain-of-fifths chromatic; the *quantization grid* is 12 equal steps per octave (which keeps the readout monotonic in elevation, the property that actually matters) while the *sounded* pitch of each step is Pythagorean. Fixed `SCAN_M_PER_OCTAVE` rather than auto-scaling each scan to the range it happens to find: auto-scaling would always use the full pitch range, but then a pitch would mean nothing on its own and flat ground would sound dramatic. Clamped rather than wrapped at `SCAN_OCTAVE_CLAMP` — a wrapped octave reports a peak as a pit. Sim scenario 2 asserts every producible pitch is a table degree **and** asserts an equal-tempered table fails the same check.
    - **The reference ping is not decoration.** An absolute-pitch elevation readout is unusable without a reference immediately beforehand, because the listener has nothing to judge "level" against. Middle C, centre, at the head of every scan, at a **fixed** level: the cloud is normalised and the ping is added *after*, or the calibration tone's loudness would become a function of the landscape it is calibrating.
    - **Water is the same pitch rule on a different timbre.** The depth rides on the **water bed**, not the surface, so "deeper is lower" falls out of the one elevation map with no second rule and the water stays pitch-continuous with the shore it meets. What marks it as water is the underscore field and timbre alone — `dz_0.0` is still definitively water, rendered as a 1/k saw against the terrain grain's near-sine. This keeps a zero-depth shoreline cell unmistakable even when its bed elevation has exactly the same pitch as adjacent dry ground.
    - **Onset jitter (`SCAN_TIME_JITTER_MS`) is required, not cosmetic.** Without it every spoke of a given ring fires on the same sample at the same pitch, so flat ground sums perfectly coherently and comes out as twenty-five rhythmic pulses — the sample rings themselves become audible as a beat, which is the exact opposite of the melded granular surface the feature exists to produce. It **must stay under half a ring's spacing in time**, or two rings can swap and time-is-distance stops being true; at `SCAN_DURATION_S` of 2.0 over the mod's 25 rings that spacing is 83 ms, so the ceiling is ~41 ms. **Shortening the scan tightens this**, which is why sim scenario 11 reads the ring count out of the Lua and asserts the margin rather than leaving it to be rediscovered. Scenario 5 separately asserts a farther sample always starts later.
    - **The cloud is normalised, so headroom is structural rather than a tuned hope.** Six hundred grains summing sixteen deep have no useful analytic peak bound. Nothing here may rely on the callback's final clip to stay clean — that clip catches the sum of every voice in the mod, and a scan arriving already at the ceiling would make every other cue distort. Only the cloud is user-levelled (`scan_tone_dbfs`); water and the object pings sit at fixed *offsets* from it, or a mix exists in which the terrain bed is inaudible under the pings.
    - **A missing sample is silence, never zero.** Zero is level ground, so rendering an off-the-map sample as 0.0 would report a flat plain where the world simply ends — a lie the listener has no way to catch. The mod sends `~` and `render_scan` skips it; sim scenario 9 asserts the naive zero-fill form produces a plateau.
    - Every grain fades in and out with a raised cosine (`SCAN_GRAIN_FADE_MS`), and a scan landing while one is still playing **crossfades** over it (`SCAN_CROSSFADE_MS`) rather than cutting — a double tap of the key is an ordinary thing to do and a hard cut mid-cloud is a click at full level.
